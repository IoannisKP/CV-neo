Agent X

How might contextual guidance help new players find their footing in Escape from Tarkov without flattening the difficulty its audience plays it for?

Agent X Contextual guidance

Help arrives after the game sees you struggle.

01 · Overview

Guidance without an easy mode.

Escape from Tarkov is deliberately demanding. Its opacity, high stakes and unforgiving systems are part of the experience, but they can also stop a newcomer before learning becomes rewarding.

This supervised student project followed one complete research cycle: interview players, synthesise the evidence, define a narrow intervention, make the idea tangible and put it in front of people. The result was not a redesign of the game. It was a restrained companion concept that appears only after repeated failure.

02 · Problem

The friction is both the feature and the risk.

New players can abandon Tarkov early. Simply lowering its difficulty would weaken the reason its established audience chooses it, so the product question was not how to make the game easier. It was how to make progress more legible.

Keep the challenge. Make the next step possible to understand.

What had to remain

The tension, discovery and player agency that make Tarkov distinct. The intervention could not solve the game on the player's behalf.

What could change

The moment after repeated failure, when a player needs enough context to form another hypothesis and try again.

03 · Research

Eight conversations across the learning curve.

I ran eight text interviews on Discord, spanning beginners through veteran players and including one person who had quit. The format produced a written record that I could compare, group and revisit during synthesis.

Interpretation 01

Difficulty needs meaning

Failure can be motivating when a player can connect it to a decision and imagine what to try next.

Interpretation 02

Timing changes the help

Guidance offered before struggle risks feeling intrusive; offered after repeated failure, it can feel earned.

Interpretation 03

Agency must survive

The intervention should create a direction, not provide a route that removes discovery.

These are design interpretations from a small qualitative study, not claims about the whole player population. They gave the concept a direction and supplied assumptions that later testing needed to challenge.

04 · Definition

Two personas, one critical moment.

The interviews were synthesised into two personas, the Seeker and the Returner, and an emotional journey map. The map shifted attention away from a generic onboarding flow and toward the point where repeated failure turns curiosity into frustration.

01

The Seeker

The target profile for the concept: a newcomer willing to learn, but at risk of losing momentum when the game gives too little context.

02

The Returner

A second profile used to keep the concept sensitive to players who come back with partial knowledge and different support needs.

03

The intervention point

The companion activates after repeated failure, when a small amount of contextual guidance can support another attempt.

05 · Concept

A companion that knows when to stay quiet.

Agent X is a diegetic companion: guidance is presented as part of the game world rather than as a detached tutorial layer. It does not begin by explaining everything. It waits for repeated failure, then offers context while leaving the next decision with the player.

Trigger

After failure

Support is conditional, so it responds to demonstrated need rather than assuming it.

Form

Inside the world

The companion keeps the guidance within the fiction and tone of the experience.

Boundary

Point, don't solve

The concept gives enough information to continue learning without taking over the task.

06 · Prototype

Four views, kept deliberately spare.

I translated the concept into four wireframe views. At this fidelity, the purpose was to test whether people could notice the intervention and understand what it was doing. It was not intended to evaluate production art, animation or final game writing.

What the prototype represented

  • A companion embedded in the game's context
  • Activation after repeated failure
  • A sequence across four wireframe views

What it did not represent

  • A finished visual interface
  • A complete onboarding system
  • Evidence that target players would want the feature

07 · Testing

The interaction read clearly. The sample did not.

Three participants completed think aloud sessions with the wireframes. They located the guidance and understood it without being pointed toward it. This was the behaviour the prototype was designed to test. The wireframes did not need to explain themselves.

What the sessions supported

The guidance was discoverable and its purpose was understandable within the wireframe sequence.

What they could not support

All three testers were aged 40 to 46 and none had played Tarkov. They did not match the Seeker profile the concept targeted.

A usable interaction is not the same as a validated product decision.

That mismatch mattered most for one screen designed to work by deliberately staying silent. Without target players who understood the surrounding game context, that decision could not be evaluated properly.

08 · Reflection

Name the weak evidence, then design the next test.

This was my first complete research cycle, run under supervision. Its clearest outcome is twofold: the interaction was understandable in the sessions, and the recruitment was not strong enough to test the concept with confidence.

01

Recruit against the persona

The next round needs actual target players who match the Seeker profile, not convenient substitutes.

02

Test the silence

The deliberately quiet view needs the context and expectations of real Tarkov players to be evaluated fairly.

03

Separate clarity from value

Finding and understanding the guidance answers a usability question. It does not yet show that the intervention belongs in the game.

The concept became clearer. So did the limit of the evidence.

Agent X produced two personas, an emotional journey, four wireframe views and an initial usability signal. Its next responsible step is targeted recruitment, not a claim that the idea is already validated.
